\paragraph{Immunization against diffusion editing.}
Image immunization against diffusion-based editing was introduced by PhotoGuard~\cite{salman2023photoguard}, which perturbs an image so that the VAE encoder or the full diffusion process of Stable Diffusion maps it to an unintended output. Subsequent methods share this formulation and differ in the component of the editor that the perturbation targets. Mist~\cite{liang2023mist} combines a textural loss on the encoder with a semantic loss on the denoiser, DIA~\cite{dia2025} attacks the inversion and reconstruction trajectory of inpainting, SIFM~\cite{dong2025sifm} perturbs intermediate features of the denoiser, and DAYN~\cite{lo2024dayn} and DANP~\cite{zhang2025danp} redirect the cross-attention of the content tokens away from the regions an instruction would edit. Further work extends the setting to instruction-guided editing, facial identity, masked inpainting, and diffusion-transformer editors~\cite{liang2023advdm,chen2024editshield,wang2025facelock,diffusionguard2025,decontext2025,nulledit2026}. Robustness to image processing has been pursued within the same additive form, by placing the perturbation in quantized JPEG coefficients~\cite{dctshield2025} or by shaping its spatial spectrum~\cite{blurguard2025}. In all of these methods the protection remains a perturbation on the pixel grid, which is the component that blurring, compression, and reconstruction attenuate. Three concurrent works continue this line. SPIN~\cite{spin2026} computes an additive perturbation by a single projection step over a random neighborhood of the image, for Stable Diffusion 3 and InstructPix2Pix. SRIM~\cite{srim2026} makes an additive perturbation robust to the rescaling that editors apply before encoding, by optimizing at each step against the weakest of a set of anchor scales, and reports robustness to input purification; its protection remains on the pixel grid and its robustness targets changes of scale. Copying as Defense~\cite{cad2026} drives the early-layer attention of diffusion-transformer editors toward the reference image so that the edit reproduces the input, and does not report an evaluation under purification.

\paragraph{Countermeasures against immunization.}
Attacks against immunization aim to remove the protection before or during editing. Common image operations such as JPEG compression and upscaling bypass several protections~\cite{sandoval2023jpeg,honig2025noisyupscaling}, and the defender is at a structural disadvantage because the protection is fixed before the attacker chooses a purification~\cite{honig2025noisyupscaling}. Learned purifiers remove protections without access to the protected images or to the defense~\cite{purifyonce2026}; among them, VAE-Trans fine-tunes a VAE encoder on clean images and reconstructs the protected image through the resulting autoencoder. Other studies remove protections on the side of the editor~\cite{cat2025} or find that protected images are often still edited as instructed~\cite{icip2025disruptive,doprotect2026}. Together, these results indicate that the effectiveness of a protection reported without purification overestimates its effectiveness against an attacker who purifies first, which makes robustness to image-space purification a necessary property of a practical defense.

\paragraph{Color-based adversarial examples.}
Besides additive perturbations, adversarial examples for image classifiers have been constructed from global color transformations, including hue and saturation shifts, per-image color lookup tables, piecewise-linear color filters, and semantic recoloring, which change the prediction of a classifier while preserving the structure of the image~\cite{hosseini2018semantic,laidlaw2019functional,zhao2020ace,shamsabadi2020colorfool,bhattad2020cadv}. Some of these attacks restrict the colors to distributions observed in natural images~\cite{yuan2022ncf}, and color filters have been found more robust than additive noise to JPEG compression and filtering~\cite{zhao2023advcf}. These results show that a function of color alone can change the response of a deep network and can persist through common image processing. Immunization faces the same processing on the attacker's side, which motivates a protection built from a global color mapping.

\paragraph{Aesthetic and quality assessment.}
Full-reference metrics compare a processed image with its original, pixel by pixel as in PSNR and CIEDE2000~\cite{sharma2005ciede2000}, by local structure as in SSIM, or in a deep feature space as in LPIPS~\cite{zhang2018lpips} and DISTS~\cite{ding2020dists}. By construction, these metrics measure fidelity to the original, so any change of color increases the distortion they report. Perceptual studies of natural images, in contrast, find that moderate changes of chroma leave perceived quality and naturalness unchanged or improve them~\cite{deridder1995naturalness,fedorovskaya1997chroma}. No-reference models assess an image without access to an original and predict its perceived quality or aesthetic appeal. NIMA~\cite{talebi2018nima} and the LAION aesthetic predictor~\cite{schuhmann2022laion} regress human aesthetic ratings, MUSIQ~\cite{ke2021musiq} predicts quality from multiple image scales, NIQE~\cite{mittal2013niqe} measures the deviation from natural-scene statistics, and CLIP-IQA~\cite{wang2023clipiqa} scores quality with vision--language prompts. For a protection that changes colors by design, a full-reference metric alone therefore cannot separate an acceptable change of color from a loss of quality. We thus evaluate distortion with LPIPS, the full-reference metric used by prior immunization work, and with no-reference aesthetic models, which judge the protected image on its own.
