\paragraph{Immunization against Diffusion Editing.}
With the growing use of diffusion models for image editing, protecting visual content against unauthorized manipulation has become an increasing concern. 
PhotoGuard~\cite{salman2023photoguard} introduces image immunization against malicious editing by adding imperceptible perturbations to the input image, disrupting either the VAE encoder or the full diffusion process of Stable Diffusion. Subsequent methods explore diverse strategies to construct effective protective perturbations by targeting different components of diffusion models, manipulating latent representations~\cite{liang2023mist,chen2024editshield}, the inversion and reconstruction process~\cite{dia2025}, and the early stage of the denoising process~\cite{diffusionguard2025}. A prominent line of work exploits attention mechanisms to interfere with the editing process, extending from U-Net-based diffusion models~\cite{lo2024dayn,zhang2025danp,advpaint2025} to more recent DiT-based editors~\cite{cad2026}.
%A prominent line of work focuses on attention mechanisms, exploiting their role in connecting visual features with semantic information to interfere with editing instructions. DAYN~\cite{lo2024dayn} and DANP~\cite{zhang2025danp} manipulate cross-attention to redirect the model's attention away from regions targeted by editing instructions, while AdvPaint~\cite{advpaint2025} explores attention-based protection in diffusion-based inpainting. More recently, Copying as Defense~\cite{cad2026} extends this direction to DiT-based editors by steering early-block attention toward preserving the input image. 
Beyond improving immunization effectiveness, recent works have investigated the robustness of protective perturbations against image processing operations. DCT-Shield~\cite{dctshield2025} optimizes perturbations in the frequency domain, while BlurGuard~\cite{blurguard2025} shapes their spatial frequency characteristics to improve resilience to blurring. SRIM~\cite{srim2026} optimizes perturbations across multiple input scales to improve robustness against resizing, and SPIN~\cite{spin2026} employs single-step projection over stochastic neighborhoods. Despite these advances, existing methods predominantly rely on additive perturbations to encode protection in the input image. In contrast, we explore an alternative paradigm that leverages color transformations for image immunization.

%Image immunization against diffusion-based editing was introduced by PhotoGuard~\cite{salman2023photoguard}, which perturbs an image so that the VAE encoder or the full diffusion process of Stable Diffusion maps it to an unintended output. Subsequent methods share this formulation and differ in the component of the editor that the perturbation targets. Mist~\cite{liang2023mist} combines a textural loss on the encoder with a semantic loss on the denoiser, DIA~\cite{dia2025} attacks the inversion and reconstruction trajectory of inpainting, SIFM~\cite{dong2025sifm} perturbs intermediate features of the denoiser, and DAYN~\cite{lo2024dayn} and DANP~\cite{zhang2025danp} redirect the cross-attention of the content tokens away from the regions an instruction would edit. Robustness to image processing has been pursued within the same additive form, by placing the perturbation in quantized JPEG coefficients~\cite{dctshield2025} or by shaping its spatial spectrum~\cite{blurguard2025}. SPIN~\cite{spin2026} computes an additive perturbation by a single projection step, SRIM~\cite{srim2026} optimizes it against several input scales so that it survives the rescaling applied by editors, and Copying as Defense~\cite{cad2026} steers the attention of diffusion-transformer editors so that the edit reproduces the input. Most of these methods carry the protection in an additive perturbation or another spatial structure on the pixel grid, the component that blurring, compression, and reconstruction attenuate, so their robustness can vary across purifications.

\paragraph{Countermeasures against Immunization.}
Countermeasures against image immunization aim to remove or bypass protection before or during editing. Since existing immunization methods predominantly encode protection through additive perturbations, a common countermeasure is image purification, which aims to remove these perturbations while preserving the original image content. Even conventional image operations, such as JPEG compression, blurring and resizing, can weaken existing protections~\cite{sandoval2023jpeg,blurguard2025,honig2025noisyupscaling}. Beyond these operations, learned purifiers can remove protection without requiring access to the original image or the immunization method. For example, VAE-Trans~\cite{purifyonce2026} fine-tunes a VAE encoder on clean images and uses the resulting autoencoder to reconstruct protected images. Other approaches bypass immunization directly within the editing pipeline~\cite{cat2025}, while empirical studies have also shown that some protected images remain editable as instructed~\cite{icip2025disruptive,doprotect2026}. 
These findings highlight that evaluating immunization without considering purification may overestimate its practical effectiveness, making robustness to image-space purification an important requirement for effective image protection.


%Attacks against immunization aim to remove the protection before or during editing. The most direct of these is purification, in which the attacker transforms the protected image before editing so that the protection is removed while the content of the image is preserved. Common image operations such as JPEG compression and upscaling already act as purifications and bypass several protections~\cite{sandoval2023jpeg,honig2025noisyupscaling}. Learned purifiers remove protections without access to the protected images or to the defense~\cite{purifyonce2026}; among them, VAE-Trans fine-tunes a VAE encoder on clean images and reconstructs the protected image through the resulting autoencoder. Other studies remove protections on the side of the editor~\cite{cat2025} or find that protected images are often still edited as instructed~\cite{icip2025disruptive,doprotect2026}. Together, these results indicate that the effectiveness of a protection reported without purification overestimates its effectiveness against an attacker who purifies first, which makes robustness to image-space purification a necessary property of a practical defense.

\paragraph{Color Transformations for Adversarial Manipulation.}
Beyond additive perturbations, prior studies have explored color transformations as an alternative means of interfering with deep neural networks for classification. Early approaches manipulate global color attributes or apply shared color transformations across pixels~\cite{hosseini2018semantic,laidlaw2019functional}, altering model predictions while preserving image structure. Subsequent methods optimize parameterized color filters~\cite{zhao2020ace,zhao2023advcf} or leverage image semantics to generate perceptually plausible adversarial recoloring~\cite{shamsabadi2020colorfool,bhattad2020cadv,yuan2022ncf}. These studies demonstrate that color transformations can effectively alter neural network behavior without relying on conventional pixel-wise perturbations, while potentially offering greater robustness to image processing operations such as JPEG compression~\cite{zhao2023advcf}. However, their primary focus is on manipulating predictions of discriminative models. In contrast, we investigate color transformations as a means of protecting visual content against unauthorized generative editing.
%Besides additive perturbations, adversarial examples for image classifiers have been constructed from global color transformations, including hue and saturation shifts, per-image color lookup tables, piecewise-linear color filters, and semantic recoloring, which change the prediction of a classifier while preserving the structure of the image~\cite{hosseini2018semantic,laidlaw2019functional,zhao2020ace,shamsabadi2020colorfool,bhattad2020cadv}. Some of these attacks restrict the colors to distributions observed in natural images~\cite{yuan2022ncf}, and color filters have been found more robust than additive noise to JPEG compression and filtering~\cite{zhao2023advcf}. These results show that a function of color alone can change the response of a deep network and can persist through common image processing. Immunization faces the same processing on the attacker's side, which motivates a protection built from a global color mapping.

%\paragraph{Aesthetic and quality assessment.}
%Full-reference metrics such as LPIPS~\cite{zhang2018lpips} measure the difference between a processed image and its original, so any change of color increases the distortion they report. No-reference aesthetic models assess an image without access to an original and are trained on human ratings of images: the LAION aesthetic predictor~\cite{schuhmann2022laion} regresses human aesthetic ratings from CLIP image embeddings, and MUSIQ~\cite{ke2021musiq}, a multi-scale image transformer, is used in its version trained on the human aesthetic ratings of the AVA dataset. These models rate the image itself and are less sensitive to whether its colors differ from those of an original. The two kinds of metric thus measure different properties of a protected image, and we report both: LPIPS for the distortion relative to the original, as in prior immunization work, and LAION and MUSIQ for the quality of the protected image.
