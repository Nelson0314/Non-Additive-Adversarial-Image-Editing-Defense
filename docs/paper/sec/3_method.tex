\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

The defense maps the colors of an image through a per-image 3D lookup table whose form is restricted to smooth rotations of color, and optimizes the table against the VAE encoder of the editor (\cref{fig:overview}).

\subsection{Threat model}
\label{sec:threat}
The defender, an image owner, transforms a photograph $x$ into a protected image $\tilde{x}$ before publication. The attacker edits $\tilde{x}$ with an instruction-guided diffusion editor $E(\cdot, p)$, where $p$ denotes a text instruction, and may first apply a purification $P$ such as JPEG compression, Gaussian blur, or reconstruction through a variational autoencoder (VAE). The defender has white-box access to InstructPix2Pix~\cite{brooks2023instructpix2pix}, with frozen weights and an unknown text prompt $p$.

The edit displacement between $E(P(\tilde{x}), p)$ and $E(P(x), p)$ is measured by the learned perceptual distance LPIPS~\cite{zhang2018lpips}, by the cosine similarity of SigLIP~\cite{zhai2023siglip} image embeddings as a measure of semantic agreement, and by the Fr\'echet inception distance (FID)~\cite{heusel2017fid} between the two sets of edits. The distortion of the protected image is the LPIPS between $\tilde{x}$ and $x$.

\subsection{Color mapping with a 3D lookup table}
\label{sec:carrier}
A 3D lookup table (LUT) represents a color mapping $T : [0,1]^3 \to [0,1]^3$ by its values on the lattice $\mathcal{L} = \{0, \tfrac{1}{32}, \dots, 1\}^3$ of $33^3$ nodes. For a pixel color $x_i$, $T(x_i)$ is the trilinear interpolation of $T$ over the eight nodes of the lattice cell that contains $x_i$, and the protected image is $\tilde{x}_i = T(x_i)$. The LUT is the standard operator of global color grading~\cite{zeng2020lut}, and its effect on an image is that of a color filter.

\subsection{Smooth color rotations}
\label{sec:feasible}
An unrestricted LUT introduces unnatural local color differences and color tones. We therefore restrict $T$ to rotations of color about the black point. With the gray axis $g = (1,1,1)/\sqrt{3}$ as the polar axis, every color is written as
\begin{equation}
  c = \|c\|\, u(\theta, \phi),
  \label{eq:spherical}
\end{equation}
where $u(\theta, \phi)$ is the unit vector with polar angle $\theta$, the deviation from the neutral axis, and azimuth $\phi$ about $g$. As in cylindrical hue--saturation color models, $\theta$ corresponds to saturation and $\phi$ to hue. The LUT preserves $\|c\|$, and thus approximately the brightness, and changes only the two angles:
\begin{equation}
  T(c) = \mathrm{clip}_{[0,1]}\big(\|c\|\, u(\theta + \Delta\theta(c),\; \phi + \Delta\phi(c))\big).
  \label{eq:rotation}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Smooth rotation field.} The free parameters $\Theta \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 33^3}$ assign a vector to each lattice node and are smoothed by a separable 3D Gaussian filter,
\begin{equation}
  V = G_\sigma * \Theta, \qquad \sigma = 4 \text{ lattice cells}.
  \label{eq:field}
\end{equation}
The filter makes $T$ smooth in color space. This keeps the gradual color transitions of skin, sky, and fabric from being mapped to distant outputs, which would produce banding; learned grading LUTs are regularized toward smoothness for the same reason~\cite{zeng2020lut}. At each node, $V$ is projected onto the unit tangent vectors $e_\theta$ and $e_\phi$ of the sphere at $u(\theta, \phi)$, giving $v_\theta = \langle V, e_\theta \rangle$ and $v_\phi = \langle V, e_\phi \rangle$.

\paragraph{Bounded angles.} The angle increments saturate smoothly at prescribed bounds,
\begin{align}
  \Delta\theta &= s_{\theta_{\max}}(v_\theta), \qquad
  \Delta\phi = s_{\phi_{\max}}\!\left(\frac{v_\phi \cos\Delta\theta}{\sin(\theta + \Delta\theta)}\right), \\
  s_a(t) &= \arctan\!\big(\tan a \, \tanh(t / \tan a)\big),
  \label{eq:bounds}
\end{align}
so that $|\Delta\theta| < \theta_{\max}$ and $|\Delta\phi| < \phi_{\max}$ before clipping, with $\Delta\phi = 0$ on the gray axis. We set $\theta_{\max} = 10^\circ$ and $\phi_{\max} = 3^\circ$. The tighter bound on hue reflects that hue shifts reduce the perceived naturalness of photographs more than chroma changes of comparable size~\cite{deridder1995naturalness,fedorovskaya1997chroma}.

Every $T$ of this form is a smooth change of saturation and hue at fixed $\|c\|$, so naturalness is a property of the parameterization, and the optimization requires no penalty term, projection, or bound on distortion. Enforcing naturalness instead through one penalty per artifact produces constraints that conflict during optimization (\cref{sec:exp_ablation}).

\subsection{Objective}
\label{sec:objective}
The table is optimized to displace the VAE latent of the protected image from that of the original. With $\mu_\mathcal{E}$ the posterior mean of the VAE encoder and $n$ the number of latent elements, the objective is
\begin{equation}
  \max_\Theta \; \frac{1}{n} \big\| \mu_\mathcal{E}(T_\Theta(x)) - \mu_\mathcal{E}(x) \big\|_2^2 .
  \label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
It requires a single forward pass through the encoder and no instruction, and its gradient involves only the VAE, which keeps the optimization tractable for editors with large denoisers such as FLUX.1 Kontext.

\subsection{Optimization}
\label{sec:optimization}
$\Theta$ is initialized with independent Gaussian entries of standard deviation 0.002, and \cref{eq:objective} is optimized with Adam ($\beta_1 = 0.5$, $\beta_2 = 0.99$) for 1800 steps at $512^2$ resolution. The learning rate follows a cosine decay from $3 \times 10^{-4}$ to $10^{-5}$ over the first two thirds of the steps and remains at $10^{-5}$ afterwards. The protected image of the final step is stored as an 8-bit PNG. One image takes 8.5 to 9 minutes on a single RTX 3090.
