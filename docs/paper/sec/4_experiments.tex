\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
Since greater perceptual distortion of the immunized image may be associated with stronger editing disruption, we compare \capg{} with existing additive defenses at comparable perceptual distortion levels, measured by LPIPS, to reduce the influence of distortion differences on the evaluation. We evaluate the protection through editing disruption, perturbation preservation, and immunization success rate (ISR), with particular emphasis on the robustness to purification.

\paragraph{Data and Editing Setup.}
\label{sec:exp_setup}
We focus on portrait images, as unauthorized manipulation of personal photographs raises particular concerns about privacy and identity misuse. Our experiments use 306 portrait images from Pexels (108 men and 198 women), used under the Pexels License. Each image contains a single, near-frontal, unoccluded face and is cropped to $512\times512$ around the face. We use InstructPix2Pix~\cite{brooks2023instructpix2pix} as the target editor. Following FaceLock~\cite{wang2025facelock}, we adopt four editing instructions: \textit{adding sunglasses, a police suit, a helmet, or a bow tie}, resulting in four editing tasks per image. The LUT uses $K = 33$ lattice nodes per channel, a Gaussian filter with $\sigma = 4$ lattice cells, and angular bounds $\theta_{\max} = 25^\circ$ and $\phi_{\max} = 7.5^\circ$; InstructPix2Pix uses Euler-Ancestral sampling with 50 steps, text guidance 7.5, image guidance 1.8, and seed 20260812.

\paragraph{Compared Baselines.}
We compare \capg{} against ten additive perturbation-based defenses: DAYN~\cite{lo2024dayn}, DIA-R and DIA-PT~\cite{dia2025}, SIFM~\cite{dong2025sifm}, DANP~\cite{zhang2025danp}, Mist~\cite{liang2023mist}, PhotoGuard (encoder attack)~\cite{salman2023photoguard}, DCT-Shield and DCT-Shield-Y~\cite{dctshield2025}, and BlurGuard~\cite{blurguard2025}. To approximately match perceptual distortion, we adjust the perturbation budgets of additive defenses based on LPIPS~\cite{zhang2018lpips}. The resulting average LPIPS values range from 0.251 to 0.318, compared with 0.287 for \capg{} (\cref{tab:main}).

\paragraph{Purification Operations.}
We consider eight purification operations applied before editing: JPEG compression (quality factors of 80, 50, and 30), Gaussian blur ($\sigma=1$ and $2$), 10\% cropping followed by resizing to the original resolution, $15^\circ$ rotation, and VAE-Trans~\cite{purifyonce2026}, a VAE-based purification method. We report VAE-Trans results separately to distinguish learned, model-based purification from conventional image transformations.

\subsection{Immunization Effectiveness under Purification}

\paragraph{Quantitative Comparison.}
\cref{tab:main} compares \capg{} with ten additive defenses at approximately matched perceptual distortion levels. Before purification (None column), several additive defenses achieve comparable or stronger editing disruption. However, they exhibit substantial performance degradation after purification. Across seven purifications, \capg{} achieves favorable edited LPIPS compared to BlurGuard, the strongest additive baseline under purification, which explicitly targets robustness to blurring. Notably, \capg{} maintains its advantage under VAE-Trans, demonstrating that its robustness extends beyond conventional image transformations to model-based purification. Consistent improvements in SigLIP~\cite{zhai2023siglip} and ArcFace~\cite{deng2019arcface} similarities further indicate that \capg{} maintains editing disruption at both semantic and facial identity levels. Meanwhile, \capg{} achieves the highest MUSIQ~\cite{ke2021musiq} score among the evaluated defenses while maintaining a comparable LAION~\cite{schuhmann2022laion} score. Together, these results demonstrate that \capg{} sustains stronger editing disruption after purification at comparable perceptual distortion levels while maintaining competitive image quality.

\paragraph{Immunization Success Rate.}
Beyond editing discrepancies, we evaluate immunization success rate (ISR), defined as the proportion of originally successful edits that fail when applied to immunized images. The evaluation uses Claude Sonnet 5.5, with a subset of judgments reviewed by humans. As shown in \cref{tab:isr}, while several additive defenses achieve higher ISR without purification, their effectiveness decreases substantially after purification. In contrast, \capg{} maintains a relatively stable ISR. Relative to BlurGuard, the strongest additive baseline after purification, \capg{} achieves a combined ISR improvement of 3.4$\pm$1.1 percentage points (95\% bootstrap interval). These results further demonstrate that \capg{} retains its ability to prevent intended edits even after purification.

\paragraph{Distortion–Effectiveness Trade-off.}
We further examine how perceptual distortion relates to editing disruption. As shown in \cref{fig:plane}(a), additive defenses generally achieve stronger editing disruption at higher distortion levels before purification, motivating our matched-distortion comparison. On the other hand, \cref{fig:plane}(b) shows that \capg{} maintains substantially stronger editing disruption than additive defenses at comparable distortion levels after purification, demonstrating a more favorable distortion–effectiveness trade-off under purification.


\paragraph{Qualitative Comparison.}
\Cref{fig:purification_example} shows the edits of one portrait with the instruction ``Let the person wear a police suit''. Without purification, the undefended image is changed into a police uniform, whereas the edits of all immunized images, including that of \capg{}, keep the original dress. After Gaussian blur, the uniform appears on the undefended image and on all five additive defenses, while the edit of \capg{} keeps the original dress with small patches on the shoulders. This agrees with the persistence analysis in \cref{sec:exp_persistence}: blur removes the additive perturbations but preserves the color mapping of \capg{}. Owing to the page limit, the ablation results, the results on an inpainting editor, and further qualitative examples are provided in the supplementary material.


\subsection{Cross-Model Transferability}
We further evaluate whether the protection against InstructPix2Pix transfers to other diffusion-based image editors. Specifically, we apply the same immunized images, optimized exclusively against InstructPix2Pix, to VIBE-DistilledCFG~\cite{alekseenko2026vibe} and FLUX.2 [klein] 4B~\cite{bfl2026flux2klein} without re-optimization. As shown in \cref{tab:transfer}, \capg{} consistently outperforms the evaluated additive defenses across both editors, even without purification, with its advantage becoming more pronounced after purification. Notably, while several additive defenses achieve stronger editing disruption on the optimization model, their effectiveness transfers less successfully to unseen editors. In contrast, \capg{} maintains strong editing disruption across different editing models under various purifications. These results demonstrate that both the editing effectiveness and purification robustness of \capg{} extend beyond the model used for optimization. Results on a mask-based inpainting editor, Stable Diffusion Inpainting, are reported in the supplementary material.

\subsection{Perturbation Persistence under Purification}
\label{sec:exp_persistence}
As the distortion–effectiveness analysis in \cref{fig:plane} shows that \capg{} achieves stronger editing disruption after purification at comparable initial distortion levels, we further investigate how purification affects the immunization introduced by different defenses in \cref{fig:purification}. Specifically, we measure the relative perceptual discrepancy after purification as $\mathrm{LPIPS}(P(\tilde{x}),P(x))/\mathrm{LPIPS}(\tilde{x},x)$, where $P$ denotes a purification operation. Additive defenses exhibit substantial reductions in this discrepancy under JPEG compression and Gaussian blur, including BlurGuard, which is designed to improve robustness. In contrast, \capg{} maintains a ratio of at least 1.0 across all evaluated JPEG and blur settings, indicating that its protection-induced differences are not attenuated by these transformations. This contrast is consistent with the global nature of the proposed color transformation, which introduces no independent spatial perturbation pattern and is therefore less susceptible to the suppression of local image variations.

\subsection{Ablation Study}
\label{sec:exp_ablation}
We evaluate the contributions of the proposed geometric constraints, and compare the proposed parameterization with alternative color mappings. Quantitative ablation results are reported in the supplementary material; component variants are evaluated without distortion matching, whereas alternative color mappings are compared at approximately matched LPIPS distortion.

\paragraph{Effect of Geometric Constraints.}
%Without optimization, randomly sampled color transformations induce minor image modifications and provide limited editing disruption. In contrast, 
\capg{} exploits the constrained color transformation space to identify more effective protection mappings while largely preserving aesthetic quality. Removing Gaussian smoothing,  relaxing the angular bounds or replacing the separate angular bounds with a single rotation bound further increases editing disruption, but at the cost of substantially greater image distortion and reduced image quality. These findings highlight the complementary roles of optimization and geometric constraints in balancing editing disruption and image quality.

\paragraph{Comparison with Alternative Color Mappings.}
At approximately matched LPIPS distortion, alternative color mappings can achieve comparable or stronger editing disruption, but consistently yield lower LAION and MUSIQ scores than \capg{}. In particular, the optimized affine transformation achieves stronger disruption under VAE-Trans but reduces LAION from 5.32 to 5.26 and MUSIQ from 5.30 to 4.80. As illustrated in the supplementary material, these alternatives introduce undesirable color shifts and artifacts, while removing the proposed constraints produces more pronounced spatial color irregularities. The proposed parameterization provides a favorable balance between editing disruption, robustness to purification, and image quality.
