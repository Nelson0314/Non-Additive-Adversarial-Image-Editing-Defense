\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

We evaluate \capg{} and additive defenses on portraits edited by InstructPix2Pix, with and without purification, and compare how much of each protection survives purification. The protected images are also applied to two editors with different encoders.

\paragraph{Data and attack.}
\label{sec:exp_setup}
All experiments use 306 portraits from Pexels (108 men, 198 women), used under the Pexels License. They are selected from a public Pexels portrait collection by automatic filters for a single, near-frontal, unoccluded color face whose apparent gender agrees with the image description, deduplicated by photographer and identity, screened visually, and cropped around the face to $512^2$. The attacker edits each image with InstructPix2Pix~\cite{brooks2023instructpix2pix} (Euler-Ancestral sampling, 50 steps, text guidance 7.5, image guidance 1.8, seed 20260812) under four instructions that add sunglasses, a police suit, a helmet, or a bow tie to the person, which yields four edits per image and defense.

\paragraph{Purification methods.}
Seven standard operations are applied before editing: JPEG compression at quality 80, 50, and 30; Gaussian blur with $\sigma = 1$ and $2$; a 10\% crop followed by resizing to the original size; and a $15^\circ$ rotation. VAE-Trans~\cite{purifyonce2026}, a fine-tuned VAE encoder used as a purifier, is reported separately as the strongest purification.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
The comparison includes ten additive methods: DAYN~\cite{lo2024dayn}, DIA-R and DIA-PT~\cite{dia2025}, SIFM~\cite{dong2025sifm}, DANP~\cite{zhang2025danp}, Mist~\cite{liang2023mist}, the encoder attack of PhotoGuard~\cite{salman2023photoguard}, DCT-Shield and DCT-Shield-Y~\cite{dctshield2025}, and BlurGuard~\cite{blurguard2025}. Each method is run at its native budget and, in the matched-distortion arm, with its budget rescaled so that the defense LPIPS on the eight-portrait set falls in 0.300--0.367; on the 306 portraits, the defense LPIPS of these arms is 0.251 to 0.318 (\cref{tab:main}).

\subsection{Results}
\label{sec:exp_results}

\paragraph{Robustness to purification.}
\label{sec:exp_anti}
Before purification, \capg{} displaces the edits as far as the additive methods at matched distortion (\cref{tab:main}). Purification reduces the displacement of every additive method, and VAE-Trans removes nearly all of it, whereas the displacement of \capg{} is unchanged on average over the seven purifications and remains the largest after VAE-Trans. The SigLIP and ArcFace similarities and the FID between defended and undefended edits follow the same pattern. The protected images of \capg{} have the highest MUSIQ score and a LAION score slightly below those of the additive methods.

\paragraph{Edit failure.}
\Cref{tab:isr} reports the immunization success rate (ISR) on 100 of the portraits: among the edits of the original image that carry out the instruction, the percentage whose counterpart on the protected image lacks the accessory, shows a different edit, or is degraded. A vision--language model (Claude) judged each edit against the original image and the undefended edit under the same purification; 606 judgments were reviewed blind by a human annotator (agreement 87.1\% on a random subset of 140), and the reviewed labels replace those of the model. Without purification, most additive methods prevent more edits than \capg{}. After purification, their rates fall, whereas the rate of \capg{} stays at its level without purification and is the highest of all methods; over the three purifications combined, its difference from BlurGuard has a 95\% interval above zero. Most requested edits still succeed for every method.

\paragraph{Distortion and displacement.}
\label{sec:exp_plane}
\Cref{fig:plane}a plots edit displacement against distortion before purification for the additive methods at their native budgets and at matched distortion. The points lie close to a single line ($r = 0.968$), and \capg{} lies on it, so before purification the displacement of a defense follows its distortion, and defenses at equal distortion are comparable. Purification moves every additive method far below this line, whereas \capg{} remains on it (\cref{fig:plane}b).

\paragraph{What purification removes.}
\label{sec:exp_purify}
\Cref{fig:purification} reports the fraction of each perturbation that survives a purification $P$, $\mathrm{LPIPS}(P(\tilde{x}), P(x)) / \mathrm{LPIPS}(\tilde{x}, x)$. Cropping and rotation leave most of every perturbation in place. JPEG compression and blur remove most of an additive perturbation and less of that of BlurGuard, and DCT-Shield-Y, whose perturbation lies in quantized JPEG coefficients, survives JPEG compression only at quality 80; it is shown at its native budget (defense LPIPS 0.629), the other additive methods at matched distortion. The perturbation of \capg{} survives the seven purifications in full on average, and most of it survives VAE-Trans.

\paragraph{Editors with different encoders.}
\label{sec:exp_gen}
InstructPix2Pix, Stable Diffusion inpainting, and Stable Diffusion 2.1 share one VAE encoder, so a defense optimized for one of them applies to all three. The lower blocks of \cref{tab:main} apply the protected images optimized on InstructPix2Pix, without re-optimization, to VIBE-DistilledCFG~\cite{alekseenko2026vibe}, which encodes images with a 32-channel DC-AE at $32\times$ downsampling (20 sampling steps), and to the distilled FLUX.2 [klein] 4B~\cite{bfl2026flux2klein} with the 32-channel FLUX.2 VAE at $8\times$ downsampling (4 sampling steps, guidance 1.0), both at $512^2$ with the four instructions phrased as additions. On both editors, \capg{} displaces the edits more than the additive methods and keeps its displacement under purification, whereas the additive methods lose most of theirs.

\subsection{Ablation study}
\label{sec:exp_ablation}
\Cref{tab:ablation} examines the pipeline on 100 of the portraits in two groups. The first group removes one component at a time: the optimization, by drawing $\Theta$ at random within the angle bounds; the Gaussian smoothing, by setting $\sigma = 0$; the angle bounds; the separate bounds on polar angle and azimuth, replaced by a single bound of $25^\circ$; and the constraint that keeps $\|c\|$ fixed. These variants are not rescaled to a common distortion, since the distortion each variant reaches is itself a result of its optimization. The second group replaces the constrained LUT with simpler color mappings whose defense LPIPS is matched to that of \capg{} on each image: a random shift of hue and saturation, an optimized global rotation of hue with a scaling of saturation, an optimized $3\times3$ affine map with offset, and a $33^3$ LUT without smoothing or angle bounds; the last three are optimized with the objective of \capg{}.

\prelim{The simpler color mappings keep less of their displacement after purification than \capg{} at the same distortion, so the optimized, bounded rotation contributes to the protection beyond the use of a color mapping.}{Sec.~4.2, version for the case that the second group falls below \capg{}.} \prelim{The simpler color mappings keep a displacement after purification comparable to that of \capg{} at the same distortion, so persistence under purification is a property of global color mappings, and the constraints of \capg{} determine the appearance of the protected image.}{Sec.~4.2, version for the case that the second group matches \capg{}; keep one of the two sentences.}
