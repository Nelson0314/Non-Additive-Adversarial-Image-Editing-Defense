\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:exp_setup}
\paragraph{Data.} The main comparison (\cref{tab:main}) uses 306 portraits from Pexels at $512^2$ resolution. \Cref{fig:plane} and \cref{fig:purification}a use the same 306 portraits; \cref{fig:purification}b--d and \cref{tab:cross,tab:ablation} use a set of eight $512^2$ portraits. Each caption states the number of images. \TODO{Selection rule and license of the Pexels portraits.}

\paragraph{Attack.} The attacker uses InstructPix2Pix~\cite{brooks2023instructpix2pix} with Euler-Ancestral sampling (50 steps, text guidance 7.5, image guidance 1.8, seed 20260812) and the four accessory instructions of FaceLock~\cite{wang2025facelock}, which yields four edits per image and defense.

\paragraph{Purification.} Seven standard operations are applied before editing: JPEG compression at quality 80, 50, and 30; Gaussian blur with $\sigma = 1$ and $2$; a 10\% crop followed by resizing to the original size; and a $15^\circ$ rotation. VAE-Trans~\cite{purifyonce2026}, a fine-tuned VAE encoder used as a purifier, is reported separately as the strongest purification.

\paragraph{Baselines.} The comparison includes nine additive methods: DAYN~\cite{lo2024dayn}, DIA-R and DIA-PT~\cite{dia2025}, SIFM~\cite{dong2025sifm}, DANP~\cite{zhang2025danp}, Mist~\cite{liang2023mist}, the encoder attack of PhotoGuard~\cite{salman2023photoguard}, and DCT-Shield and DCT-Shield-Y~\cite{dctshield2025}. Each method is run at its native budget and, in the matched-distortion arm, with its budget rescaled so that the defense LPIPS on the eight-portrait set falls in 0.300--0.367.

\paragraph{Metrics.} Distortion is the LPIPS between the protected and the original image; PSNR and SSIM~\cite{wang2004ssim} on the same pair are reported alongside. Because full-reference metrics count a change of color as degradation, image quality is assessed with four no-reference aesthetic scores: LAION~\cite{schuhmann2022laion}, NIMA~\cite{talebi2018nima}, MUSIQ~\cite{ke2021musiq}, and NIQE~\cite{mittal2013niqe}. Edit displacement is measured by LPIPS and SigLIP similarity (\cref{sec:threat}), by the CLIP~\cite{radford2021clip} similarity between the two edits (CLIP-I), and by the FID between the sets of defended and undefended edits. Whether the edit still follows the instruction is measured by the CLIP similarity between the edit and the instruction text (CLIP-S)~\cite{hessel2021clipscore}, and the preservation of the subject's identity by the ArcFace similarity between the two edits. Retention is the ratio of edit LPIPS after and before purification, computed per edit and averaged. DISTS and a color-aligned distortion are reported in the supplementary material.

\subsection{Robustness to purification}
\label{sec:exp_anti}
\Cref{tab:main} reports the comparison on the 306 Pexels portraits. Before purification, the additive methods displace the edits by 0.262 to 0.415 LPIPS and our defense by 0.277. Averaged over the seven purifications, the displacement of the additive methods falls to 0.112 to 0.212, whereas ours remains at 0.291 with a retention of 1.091. Under the weakest purification of each method, Gaussian blur ($\sigma = 2$) for every additive method and rotation for ours, the additive methods retain 0.028 to 0.106 LPIPS and our defense 0.251. After VAE-Trans, the strongest purification considered, our defense retains \NUM{} against \NUM{} for the additive methods. The no-reference aesthetic scores of our protected images (\cref{tab:aesthetics}) are \NUM{} relative to those of the additive methods.

\Cref{fig:plane}b places the matched-distortion arms on the plane of distortion and displacement. Before purification, every defense lies close to the line fitted to the additive methods at all budgets. Purification moves every additive method far below this line, whereas our defense remains on it. On the eight-portrait set, the FID between defended and undefended edits after purification shows the same separation: 70.6 for our defense against 26.9--51.3 for the additive methods on average over the seven purifications, and 64.9 against 8.2--19.3 after VAE-Trans.

\subsection{Distortion and displacement before purification}
\label{sec:exp_plane}
\Cref{fig:plane}a plots edit displacement before purification against distortion for the additive methods at their native budgets and at the matched-distortion arm. On the 306 portraits, the 18 points follow a single line, edit LPIPS $= 1.037 \times$ defense LPIPS $+ 0.049$ ($r = 0.967$). At the native budgets, the defense LPIPS ranges from 0.280 to 0.629, and the ranking of the methods by displacement follows their ranking by distortion. On the eight-portrait set, budget sweeps of five methods follow the same form, each along its own line ($r = 0.987$ to $1.000$), and replacing LPIPS on the horizontal axis with DISTS or PieAPP preserves the linearity within each method ($r = 0.87$ to $0.99$) but not a common slope. Our defense lies 0.009 above the pooled LPIPS line. Before purification, the displacement of a defense is therefore determined largely by its distortion, independently of the form of the perturbation, and defenses of equal distortion are comparable.

\subsection{What purification removes}
\label{sec:exp_purify}
To separate the effect of purification on the protected image from its effect on the edit, \cref{fig:purification}a reports the fraction of the perturbation that survives a purification $P$, $\mathrm{LPIPS}(P(\tilde{x}), P(x)) / \mathrm{LPIPS}(\tilde{x}, x)$. Cropping and rotation leave most of every perturbation in place. JPEG compression, blur, and VAE-Trans separate the two kinds of defense: On the 306 portraits, Gaussian blur with $\sigma = 2$ leaves 4--16\% of an additive perturbation, whereas our perturbation survives the seven purifications in full on average (1.097). \NUM{VAE-Trans survival on the 306 portraits.}

\Cref{fig:purification}b--d relate the strength of a purification to the remaining edit displacement. For the additive methods, the displacement decreases monotonically with JPEG quality, blur radius, and downscaling, and reaches 0.03--0.14 at the strongest settings, close to the displacement of 0.109 between two seeds on the same undefended image. Our defense remains between 0.345 and 0.437 over all settings and retains 0.95--1.27 of its unpurified displacement; stronger JPEG compression increases it from 0.377 at quality 95 to 0.437 at quality 10. DCT-Shield, which places its perturbation in quantized JPEG coefficients, loses most of its displacement already at quality 95 (0.072, from 0.322 without purification) and remains at that level as the quality decreases.

\subsection{Editors with different encoders}
\label{sec:exp_gen}
InstructPix2Pix, Stable Diffusion inpainting, and Stable Diffusion 2.1 share the same VAE encoder, so a defense optimized for one of them applies to all three. FLUX.1 Kontext~\cite{flux2025kontext} and UltraEdit~\cite{zhao2024ultraedit}, which builds on Stable Diffusion 3, use different 16-channel VAEs. Defenses optimized on InstructPix2Pix and evaluated on these two editors preserve the ranking of the methods (Spearman correlation 0.951). \Cref{tab:cross} additionally reports defenses optimized against the encoder of each editor. Only methods whose loss involves the encoder alone can be transferred with their original objective, namely the PhotoGuard encoder attack, DCT-Shield, DCT-Shield-Y, and the textural term of Mist; methods that require gradients through the denoiser exceed the available memory for FLUX.1 Kontext. Before purification, these defenses displace the edits of both editors by 0.41--0.61 at their native budgets. VAE-Trans reduces the displacement of the PhotoGuard encoder attack and the two DCT-Shield variants to 0.06--0.16, whereas the textural term of Mist retains 0.30--0.32 at a defense LPIPS of 0.52--0.60. Our defense optimized against each encoder retains 0.370 on UltraEdit and 0.392 on FLUX.1 Kontext on average over the seven purifications, and 0.264 and 0.315 after VAE-Trans, at a defense LPIPS of 0.269--0.301, below that of the additive methods (0.41--0.61). The table optimized on InstructPix2Pix and applied without re-optimization yields 0.371 and 0.418 on average over the seven purifications and 0.263 and 0.327 after VAE-Trans. Because our defense has no bound on distortion, its defense LPIPS differs between the rows of \cref{tab:cross}.

\subsection{Ablations}
\label{sec:exp_ablation}
\Cref{tab:ablation} varies how naturalness is enforced and how the rotation is bounded. Formulations that impose the appearance constraints during optimization, as penalties or as a projection onto a constraint set, retain 0.302--0.363 of edit LPIPS on average over the seven purifications and 0.185--0.276 after VAE-Trans. The rotation parameterization retains 0.342--0.389 and 0.281--0.329, with a retention of 1.02--1.07. The penalty formulation satisfies all constraints at 76 of 180 checks during optimization on average, and the projection onto an image-adaptive constraint set at 109. Among the rotations, a single bound of $6^\circ$ on the rotation angle yields the largest displacement, while separate bounds on saturation and hue keep the LAION score at the level of the original image (5.48 against 5.38). A random rotation field drawn from the same parameterization without optimization, with its magnitude scaled so that each image reaches the defense LPIPS of our defense, displaces the edits by 0.366 before purification, 0.368 on average over the seven purifications, and 0.285 after VAE-Trans, against 0.374, 0.367, and 0.301 for the optimized table; at the same distortion, the random table lowers the LAION score of the protected image from 5.48 to 5.38.
