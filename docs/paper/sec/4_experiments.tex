\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

We compare \capg{} with additive defenses at matched distortion and measure, before and after purification, how much of each perturbation is preserved, how far the edits of the protected image differ from the edits of the original, and how often a requested edit fails.

\paragraph{Data and attack.}
\label{sec:exp_setup}
All experiments use 306 portraits from Pexels (108 men, 198 women), used under the Pexels License, each with a single, near-frontal, unoccluded face and cropped around the face to $512^2$. The attacker edits each image with InstructPix2Pix~\cite{brooks2023instructpix2pix} (Euler-Ancestral sampling, 50 steps, text guidance 7.5, image guidance 1.8, seed 20260812) under four instructions that add sunglasses, a police suit, a helmet, or a bow tie to the person, which yields four edits per image and defense.

\paragraph{Purification methods.}
Seven standard operations are applied before editing: JPEG compression at quality 80, 50, and 30; Gaussian blur with $\sigma = 1$ and $2$; a 10\% crop followed by resizing to the original size; and a $15^\circ$ rotation. VAE-Trans~\cite{purifyonce2026}, a pretrained VAE-based denoiser, is reported separately as the strongest purification.

\paragraph{Compared methods.}
The comparison includes ten additive methods: DAYN~\cite{lo2024dayn}, DIA-R and DIA-PT~\cite{dia2025}, SIFM~\cite{dong2025sifm}, DANP~\cite{zhang2025danp}, Mist~\cite{liang2023mist}, the encoder attack of PhotoGuard~\cite{salman2023photoguard}, DCT-Shield and DCT-Shield-Y~\cite{dctshield2025}, and BlurGuard~\cite{blurguard2025}. Each method is run at its native budget and, in the matched-distortion arm, with its budget rescaled so that the defense LPIPS on the eight-portrait set falls in 0.300--0.367; on the 306 portraits, the defense LPIPS of these arms is 0.251 to 0.318 (\cref{tab:main}).

\subsection{Results}
\label{sec:exp_results}

\paragraph{Perturbation survival.}
\label{sec:exp_purify}
\Cref{fig:purification} reports the fraction of each perturbation that is preserved by a purification $P$, $\mathrm{LPIPS}(P(\tilde{x}), P(x)) / \mathrm{LPIPS}(\tilde{x}, x)$. Cropping and rotation preserve most of every perturbation. JPEG compression and blur remove most of the additive perturbations and less of that of BlurGuard, whereas the perturbation of \capg{} is preserved in full. After VAE-Trans, 0.83 of the perturbation of \capg{} remains, against 0.03 to 0.35 for the additive methods. This behavior is consistent with the form of the two kinds of perturbation: the purifications considered here primarily attenuate high-frequency spatial content, where additive perturbations concentrate, whereas a global color mapping introduces no spatial pattern of its own.

\paragraph{Edit displacement after purification.}
\label{sec:exp_anti}
\Cref{tab:main} reports the difference between the edit of the protected image and the edit of the original under the same purification. Under purification, the difference decreases for every additive method and is unchanged for \capg{} (0.353 without purification, 0.364 on average over the seven purifications); after VAE-Trans, it is 0.288 for \capg{}, against at most 0.143 for the additive methods. The perturbation that purification preserves thus still reaches the edit. Among the protected images, those of \capg{} have a comparable LPIPS, the lowest LAION score, and a MUSIQ score at the top of the range.

\paragraph{Distortion and displacement.}
\label{sec:exp_plane}
\Cref{fig:plane}a plots the edit difference before purification against the defense LPIPS for the additive methods at their native budgets and at matched distortion. The points lie close to a single line ($r = 0.968$), and \capg{} lies 0.006 LPIPS above it, so the comparison in \cref{tab:main} is made at comparable distortion. Averaged over the seven purifications, the additive methods fall 0.06 (BlurGuard) to 0.23 LPIPS below the value that this line gives at their distortion, whereas \capg{} lies 0.02 above it (\cref{fig:plane}b).

\paragraph{Edit failure rate.}
\label{sec:exp_isr}
\Cref{tab:isr} reports the immunization success rate (ISR) on 100 of the portraits: among the edits of the original image that carry out the instruction, the percentage whose counterpart on the protected image lacks the accessory, shows a different edit, or is degraded, as judged by Claude Sonnet~5.5 with human review of a sample of the judgments. Without purification, \capg{} does not reach a higher ISR than the additive methods. Under purification, the ISR of \capg{} is preserved, at 17.0\% without purification and 16.5$\pm$2.8\% over the three purifications combined, whereas that of the additive methods decreases to between 2.6\% and 12.2\%. After purification, \capg{} exceeds BlurGuard, the closest additive method, by 4.3$\pm$2.5\%; after JPEG compression alone the difference is 1.3$\pm$3.2\%.

\paragraph{Editors with different encoders.}
\label{sec:exp_gen}
The lower blocks of \cref{tab:main} apply the protected images optimized against the VAE encoder of InstructPix2Pix, without re-optimization, to VIBE-DistilledCFG~\cite{alekseenko2026vibe} (DC-AE encoder, 20 sampling steps) and to the distilled FLUX.2 [klein] 4B~\cite{bfl2026flux2klein} (FLUX.2 VAE, 4 sampling steps, guidance 1.0), both at $512^2$ with the four instructions phrased as additions. On both editors, the edit difference of \capg{} is unchanged by purification (0.318 and 0.331 on VIBE-DistilledCFG, 0.281 and 0.293 on FLUX.2 [klein]), whereas that of the additive methods decreases.

\subsection{Ablation study}
\label{sec:exp_ablation}
\Cref{tab:ablation} compares \capg{} on the 100 portraits of \cref{tab:isr} with two groups of variants. The first group changes one component and is not matched in distortion. A random $\Theta$ draws the parameters from a standard normal distribution without optimization. $\sigma = 0$ removes the Gaussian smoothing, so that each lattice node rotates independently. Without angular bounds, $v_\theta$ and $v_\phi / r$ are used without $s_a$. A single bound of $25^\circ$ on the rotation angle of each color replaces the separate bounds on $\theta$ and $\phi$. The second group replaces the parameterization with simpler color mappings, optimized with the same objective and matched per portrait to the protected-image LPIPS of \capg{}. The hue and saturation change rotates all colors about the gray axis by one angle and scales their saturation by one factor, with random or optimized values. The affine transform maps $c$ to $Ac + b$ with 12 parameters. The unconstrained LUT optimizes all $33^3$ lattice values without smoothing, bounds, or norm preservation. The parameters of the optimized hue and saturation change and of the affine transform are scaled by 10 and 3, respectively, so that the target LPIPS is reached in the same number of optimization steps. Quality is scored by the no-reference aesthetic models LAION~\cite{schuhmann2022laion} and MUSIQ~\cite{ke2021musiq}; differences to \capg{} are paired over the 99 portraits common to all rows, with 95\% bootstrap intervals. All color variants preserve their edit displacement under purification.

At a protected-image LPIPS of about 0.30 (\cref{fig:ablation_appearance}, bottom), \capg{} keeps the skin tone close to that of the original and adds pale green patches to the foliage and grass in the background. The optimized hue and saturation change turns the skin and the background green and the denim purple; its MUSIQ and LAION scores differ from those of \capg{} by $-0.19 \pm 0.07$ and $-0.12 \pm 0.05$. The $3\times3$ affine transform adds an orange cast and lowers the contrast of the whole image ($-0.49 \pm 0.08$ and $-0.04 \pm 0.05$). The unconstrained LUT keeps the skin tone and adds purple patches to the dark foliage ($-0.28 \pm 0.07$ and $-0.06 \pm 0.05$). The random hue and saturation change does not reach this LPIPS on 34 portraits.

Removing the smoothing, the angular bounds, or the separate bounds on $\theta$ and $\phi$ raises the protected-image LPIPS by 0.11 to 0.17. On the portrait of \cref{fig:ablation_appearance} (top), without smoothing the face, the hands, and the background show irregular color patches with sharp boundaries and rainbow-colored contours (MUSIQ $-0.28 \pm 0.05$); without the angular bounds the skin turns yellow-green and the hair and the trees magenta ($-0.39 \pm 0.06$); a single bound of $25^\circ$ produces the same shift with a smaller extent ($-0.17 \pm 0.05$). A random $\Theta$ changes the image little, with a protected-image LPIPS of 0.045.
