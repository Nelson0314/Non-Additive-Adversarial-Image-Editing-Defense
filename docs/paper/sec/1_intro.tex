\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Recent advances in diffusion-based image generation and editing~\cite{rombach2022ldm,brooks2023instructpix2pix} have made it increasingly easy to manipulate images through natural-language instructions. While these capabilities enable flexible and accessible image editing, they also lower the barrier to unauthorized manipulation of publicly shared images, raising growing concerns about content authenticity and individual privacy. To mitigate these risks, image immunization~\cite{salman2023photoguard} has emerged as a proactive defense that allows image owners to introduce visually constrained modifications before releasing their images, aiming to disrupt subsequent unauthorized editing. 

Most existing image immunization methods achieve protection by introducing small additive adversarial perturbations to the original image, aiming to disrupt unauthorized editing while keeping the modifications visually imperceptible~\cite{salman2023photoguard,liang2023mist,dia2025,dctshield2025}. Despite their effectiveness against direct editing, these perturbations can be substantially weakened or removed by purification operations such as JPEG compression, image rescaling, and VAE-based reconstruction~\cite{sandoval2023jpeg,honig2025noisyupscaling,purifyonce2026}. Although recent methods have explored frequency-domain optimization, spatial smoothing, and multi-scale training to improve robustness~\cite{dctshield2025,blurguard2025,srim2026}, they largely retain the additive perturbation formulation. Rather than solely improving the robustness of additive perturbations, in this work, we explore an alternative direction: \textit{can image immunization take a different form that is less susceptible to purification?}

To answer this question, we explore color transformations as an alternative form of image immunization. Unlike conventional additive perturbations that independently modify pixel values, a global color transformation applies a consistent mapping to pixels with the same color, without introducing spatially varying perturbation patterns. It allows protective modifications to be expressed as changes in the image's color appearance rather than fine-grained pixel-level noise, offering a potentially more persistent form of protection against purification.

Building on this insight, we propose Color-based Anti-Purification Image Immunization, which learns an image-specific 3D color lookup table (LUT) to disrupt diffusion-based image editing through global color transformations. While adversarial color transformations have previously been explored for attacking image classifiers~\cite{laidlaw2019functional,zhao2023advcf,yuan2022ncf}, image immunization presents a distinct challenge: the protected image must retain its visual quality and remain suitable for public sharing while effectively disrupting subsequent editing. Unconstrained color transformations may introduce disruptive color shifts and visible artifacts, limiting their practical applicability. To address this challenge, we introduce a geometry-guided color transformation that preserves the magnitude of RGB color vectors while enforcing smoothness and bounded angular displacements around the neutral gray axis. The proposed constraints encourage visually coherent, filter-like color adjustments rather than conspicuous adversarial artifacts, making the protected images more suitable for practical use.

We conduct extensive experiments to evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed approach in disrupting diffusion-based image editing and the persistence of its protection under purification. Our results show that, compared with existing additive perturbation-based defenses, our approach better preserves its protective effectiveness under common purification operations, including JPEG compression, image rescaling, and VAE-based reconstruction. Moreover, our method demonstrates stronger cross-model transferability under purification, maintaining its protective effectiveness against unseen diffusion-based editors. We further examine the trade-off between visual distortion and editing disruption through comparisons at matched distortion levels. Finally, qualitative results and aesthetic evaluations demonstrate that our method produces visually coherent, filter-like color adjustments while maintaining the overall visual quality of the protected images.

Our main contributions are summarized as follows:

\begin{itemize}
    \item We introduce an alternative protection paradigm that leverages image-specific global color transformations instead of conventional additive perturbations, aiming to improve the persistence of image immunization under purification.
    \item We propose a constrained 3D LUT parameterization with smooth and bounded color rotations, enabling effective immunization while maintaining visually coherent appearance.
    \item We demonstrate improved protection persistence under common purification operations and stronger cross-model transferability after purification. 
    
\end{itemize}

The LUT rotates each color vector about the black point, with the gray axis as the polar axis. The polar angle, which governs saturation, and the azimuth, which governs hue, each change within a small bound, and the rotation varies smoothly across color space (\cref{sec:feasible}).
