\begin{abstract}
Image immunization protects publicly released images against unauthorized generative editing by introducing adversarial modifications before publication. However, existing defenses predominantly rely on additive pixel perturbations, whose effectiveness often deteriorates under common purification operations such as JPEG compression, Gaussian blur, and learned image reconstruction. We propose \capg{}, a color-based immunization framework that moves beyond additive perturbations by embedding protection into a per-image optimized color lookup table. By constraining color transformations through smooth, bounded rotations around the gray axis, \capg{} disrupts unauthorized edits while maintaining image quality. Unlike spatially additive perturbations, the proposed color mapping introduces no independent spatial perturbation pattern, making its protection more resistant to conventional image purifications. Experiments demonstrate that \capg{} maintains substantially stronger editing disruption and higher immunization success rates after purification, including learned VAE-based reconstruction, while preserving competitive image quality. Moreover, its effectiveness transfers to unseen editing models without re-optimization. Our results highlight the potential of color transformations to expand the design space of purification-robust image immunization.

%Recent generative image models such as Stable Diffusion allow a publicly released photograph, portraits in particular, to be modified with a text prompt, which raises privacy and security concerns. Image immunization addresses this threat by modifying a photograph before its release. Most existing methods superimpose an additive adversarial perturbation on the image, which reduces the chance that an edit succeeds. Purification operations such as JPEG compression, Gaussian blurring, and encoder-based reconstruction remove it with little effort. This vulnerability stems from the spatial structure of the perturbation. We propose \capg{}, a filter-like defense that encodes the protection in the colors of the image. Each image receives its own 3D color lookup table (LUT), the operator of global color grading. Acting on colors alone, the LUT introduces no spatial structure into the image, and the protection it carries persists through purification. The protected image appears as a color-graded version of the original. On InstructPix2Pix at matched distortion, the edit displacement retained after purification, averaged over seven standard purifications, is 1.32 to 3.23 times that of additive defenses.
\end{abstract}
