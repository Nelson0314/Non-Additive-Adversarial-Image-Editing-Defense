\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:exp_setup}
\paragraph{Data.} All experiments use 306 portraits from Pexels at $512^2$ resolution. \TODO{Selection rule and license of the Pexels portraits.}

\paragraph{Attack.} The attacker uses InstructPix2Pix~\cite{brooks2023instructpix2pix} with Euler-Ancestral sampling (50 steps, text guidance 7.5, image guidance 1.8, seed 20260812) and the four accessory instructions of FaceLock~\cite{wang2025facelock}, which yields four edits per image and defense. In \cref{sec:exp_gen}, UltraEdit~\cite{zhao2024ultraedit} runs at $512^2$ resolution with text guidance 2.5, image guidance 1.5, and 50 sampling steps, and FLUX.1 Kontext~\cite{flux2025kontext} at $512^2$ resolution with guidance 3.5, 28 sampling steps, and NF4 quantization.

\paragraph{Purification.} Seven standard operations are applied before editing: JPEG compression at quality 80, 50, and 30; Gaussian blur with $\sigma = 1$ and $2$; a 10\% crop followed by resizing to the original size; and a $15^\circ$ rotation. VAE-Trans~\cite{purifyonce2026}, a fine-tuned VAE encoder used as a purifier, is reported separately as the strongest purification.

\paragraph{Baselines.} The comparison includes ten additive methods: DAYN~\cite{lo2024dayn}, DIA-R and DIA-PT~\cite{dia2025}, SIFM~\cite{dong2025sifm}, DANP~\cite{zhang2025danp}, Mist~\cite{liang2023mist}, the encoder attack of PhotoGuard~\cite{salman2023photoguard}, DCT-Shield and DCT-Shield-Y~\cite{dctshield2025}, and BlurGuard~\cite{blurguard2025}. Each method is run at its native budget and, in the matched-distortion arm, with its budget rescaled so that the defense LPIPS on the eight-portrait set falls in 0.300--0.367.

\paragraph{Metrics.} Distortion is the LPIPS between the protected and the original image. Because full-reference metrics count a change of color as degradation, image quality is assessed with the no-reference aesthetic scores LAION~\cite{schuhmann2022laion} and MUSIQ~\cite{ke2021musiq}; PSNR and SSIM against the original, NIMA~\cite{talebi2018nima}, and NIQE~\cite{mittal2013niqe} are reported in the supplementary material. Edit displacement is measured by LPIPS and SigLIP similarity (\cref{sec:threat}), by SSIM~\cite{wang2004ssim} and PSNR between the two edits, by the CLIP~\cite{radford2021clip} similarity between the two edits (CLIP-I), and by the FID between the sets of defended and undefended edits. Whether the edit still follows the instruction is measured by the CLIP similarity between the edit and the instruction text (CLIP-S)~\cite{hessel2021clipscore}, and the preservation of the subject's identity by the ArcFace similarity between the two edits. Retention is the ratio of edit LPIPS after and before purification, computed per edit and averaged. DISTS and a color-aligned distortion are reported in the supplementary material.

\subsection{Robustness to purification}
\label{sec:exp_anti}
\Cref{tab:main} reports the comparison on the 306 Pexels portraits. Before purification, the additive methods displace the edits by 0.262 to 0.415 LPIPS and our defense by 0.277. Averaged over the seven purifications, the displacement of the additive methods falls to 0.112 to 0.212, whereas ours remains at 0.291 with a retention of 1.091. The 95\% interval of this mean across the seven purifications has a half-width of 0.056 to 0.068 for the additive methods, 27\% to 61\% of their means, and 0.025 for ours, 9\% of its mean (\cref{tab:main}), so the displacement of our defense varies less between purifications; the results for each purification are given in the supplementary material. After VAE-Trans, the strongest purification considered, our defense retains 0.218 against \prelim{0.023--0.056}{Range over the nine additive methods; BlurGuard not yet included.} for the additive methods. Our protected images have a LAION score of 5.388, within the range of the additive methods (5.345--5.531), and the highest MUSIQ score (5.343, against 5.117--5.298).

\Cref{fig:plane}b places the matched-distortion arms on the plane of distortion and displacement. Before purification, every defense lies close to the line fitted to the additive methods at all budgets. Purification moves every additive method far below this line, whereas our defense remains on it. The FID between defended and undefended edits after purification shows the same separation: \prelim{70.6}{} for our defense against \prelim{26.9--51.3}{} for the additive methods on average over the seven purifications, and \prelim{64.9}{} against \prelim{8.2--19.3}{FID values from the 8-portrait set without BlurGuard; the 306-portrait FID is not yet available.} after VAE-Trans.

\subsection{Distortion and displacement before purification}
\label{sec:exp_plane}
\Cref{fig:plane}a plots edit displacement before purification against distortion for the additive methods at their native budgets and at the matched-distortion arm. On the 306 portraits, the \prelim{18}{} points follow a single line, edit LPIPS $= \prelim{1.037}{} \times$ defense LPIPS $+ \prelim{0.049}{}$ ($r = {}$\prelim{0.967}{Point count and fit without the two BlurGuard points.}). At the native budgets, the defense LPIPS ranges from 0.280 to 0.629, and the ranking of the methods by displacement follows their ranking by distortion. On the eight-portrait set, budget sweeps of five methods follow the same form, each along its own line ($r = 0.987$ to $1.000$), and replacing LPIPS on the horizontal axis with DISTS or PieAPP preserves the linearity within each method ($r = 0.87$ to $0.99$) but not a common slope. Our defense lies 0.009 above the pooled LPIPS line, and with both angle bounds multiplied by 2.5 its operating point moves to a defense LPIPS of \prelim{0.302}{} and an edit LPIPS of \prelim{0.367}{82 Pexels portraits; the 306-image run is in progress.}, \prelim{0.005}{} above the line. Before purification, the displacement of a defense is therefore determined largely by its distortion, independently of the form of the perturbation, and defenses of equal distortion are comparable.

\subsection{What purification removes}
\label{sec:exp_purify}
To separate the effect of purification on the protected image from its effect on the edit, \cref{fig:purification}a reports the fraction of the perturbation that survives a purification $P$, $\mathrm{LPIPS}(P(\tilde{x}), P(x)) / \mathrm{LPIPS}(\tilde{x}, x)$. Cropping and rotation leave most of every perturbation in place. JPEG compression, blur, and VAE-Trans separate the two kinds of defense: On the 306 portraits, Gaussian blur with $\sigma = 2$ leaves 4--16\% of an additive perturbation, and DCT-Shield-Y at its native budget, which places its perturbation in quantized JPEG coefficients, keeps its perturbation under JPEG compression at quality 80 (1.02) but retains 36\% and 25\% at quality 50 and 30, whereas our perturbation survives the seven purifications in full on average (1.097). After VAE-Trans, \prelim{--}{} of our perturbation survives, against \prelim{--}{VAE-Trans survival on the 306 portraits not yet computed.} of the additive perturbations.

\Cref{fig:purification}b--d relate the strength of a purification to the remaining edit displacement. For the additive methods, the displacement decreases monotonically with JPEG quality, blur radius, and downscaling, and reaches \prelim{0.03--0.19}{} at the strongest settings, close to the displacement of \prelim{0.109}{} between two seeds on the same undefended image. Our defense remains between \prelim{0.345}{} and \prelim{0.437}{} over all settings and retains \prelim{0.95--1.27}{} of its unpurified displacement; stronger JPEG compression increases it from \prelim{0.377}{} at quality 95 to \prelim{0.437}{Strength sweep on 8 portraits (\texttt{baseline/results/sweeps/ip2p/purifier\_strength/retention.csv}).} at quality 10. DCT-Shield-Y at its native budget keeps most of its displacement down to quality 70 (\prelim{0.538}{}, from \prelim{0.626}{} without purification) and falls to \prelim{0.306}{} at quality 50 and \prelim{0.190}{8 portraits, native budget.} at quality 10.

\subsection{Editors with different encoders}
\label{sec:exp_gen}
InstructPix2Pix, Stable Diffusion inpainting, and Stable Diffusion 2.1 share the same VAE encoder, so a defense optimized for one of them applies to all three. FLUX.1 Kontext~\cite{flux2025kontext} and UltraEdit~\cite{zhao2024ultraedit}, which builds on Stable Diffusion 3, use different 16-channel VAEs. \Cref{tab:cross} applies the protected images of the PhotoGuard encoder attack, DCT-Shield, DCT-Shield-Y, Mist, and ours, optimized on InstructPix2Pix at matched distortion, to these two editors (settings in \cref{sec:exp_setup}). The defenses preserve the ranking of the methods (Spearman correlation \prelim{0.951}{Ranking correlation from the earlier 8-portrait run; to be recomputed on the Pexels set.}). Averaged over the seven purifications, the additive methods displace the edits by \prelim{0.097--0.135}{} on UltraEdit and \prelim{0.107--0.149}{} on FLUX.1 Kontext, and our table by \prelim{0.284}{} and \prelim{0.292}{UltraEdit: 50 Pexels portraits; FLUX.1 Kontext: 34 Pexels portraits.}, with a retention of \prelim{1.121}{} and \prelim{1.019}{}. After VAE-Trans, our table retains \prelim{0.207}{} on UltraEdit and \prelim{0.260}{} on FLUX.1 Kontext, against \prelim{0.016--0.024}{} and \prelim{0.060--0.074}{} for the additive methods. Relative to InstructPix2Pix, the edit LPIPS of our defense averaged over the seven purifications changes by a factor of \prelim{0.98}{} on UltraEdit and \prelim{1.00}{} on FLUX.1 Kontext, against \prelim{0.82--0.89}{} and \prelim{0.91--1.03}{Ratios of the 50-portrait (UltraEdit) and 34-portrait (FLUX.1 Kontext) means to the 306-portrait InstructPix2Pix means; not computed on the same subset.} for the additive methods, whose displacement on the two editors stays at or below \prelim{0.149}{}.

No variant is rescaled to the distortion of the full pipeline, since the distortion each variant reaches is itself a result of its optimization.
